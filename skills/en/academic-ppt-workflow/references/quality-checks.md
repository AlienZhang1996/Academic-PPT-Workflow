# Acceptance Criteria

Read the section relevant to the current stage. Methods are in [Visual production](visual-production.md), and record formats are in [Review and change records](project-templates.md#review-and-change-records). Distinguish pass, fail, unverified, and not applicable for each item; explain why an item is not applicable. Refer only to the [main workflow](../SKILL.md#confirmation-and-scope-of-changes) for authorization and pause rules. Review records do not replace user confirmation.

## Content and logic acceptance

- **Whole deck:** Goals, audience, and section responsibilities align. Distinguish hard limits, approximate targets, and self-selected scale. Meet hard constraints. If the first draft reasonably exceeds an approximate target to provide a complete explanation, give the reason and an honest time estimate; do not remove necessary content to meet a self-imposed slide count. Check each section's central question and audience task; a section title containing the relevant words does not establish sound grouping. Use actual evidence to decide whether independent evaluation, response, or decision tasks need their own sections. Check user requirements and the main workflow's default structure before checking the outline; a self-shortened outline cannot prove completeness. Every main-content and concluding slide has a clear place. Check agenda placement and transitions for multislide summary/reflection blocks too. Standalone section slides correspond one-to-one to the agenda in multisection decks, with combinations/omissions following the main workflow's exceptions.
- **Within sections:** Read titles and slide roles in the actual order. Determine why each preceding slide leads to the current one, and look for repetition, skipped steps, or unexplained prerequisites. Map parallel topics promised by the agenda/overview to their detailed slides and check a shared framework and comparable depth. Labeling the deck as “focused on one option,” or putting other branches only in notes, does not replace complete slides. Check user-limited scopes and background-only mentions according to their actual scope.
- **Within slides:** Titles, questions, and conclusions serve distinct roles. For each group of points, assess how they answer the slide's purpose, whether parallel points use shared dimensions and comparable levels, whether progressions include intermediate steps, and whether annotations are subordinate to the right content. Arrows, grouping, and layout must express actual relationships. The presence of all keywords does not establish those relationships.
- **Content completeness:** The master record contains all on-slide text, graphical labels/relationships, necessary qualifications, and notes. Do not give a keyword-only outline to an image model to fill in.
- **Explanatory adequacy:** Check required information under the applicable [Content patterns](content-patterns.md#pattern-index). “One topic per slide” or “four points on each slide” does not substitute for this check. Each method needing a worked example maps to a concrete input/scenario, intermediate changes, result, and principle steps. A combined exercise, blank question mark, or instruction to “give some examples” does not supply an unshown process. Combining an example with its principle or omitting it requires a basis in audience, content, or user scope; approximate timing alone does not justify deleting necessary examples.
- **Consistency within a group:** Parallel topics use comparable title structures, terminology, explanatory dimensions, and order to support side-by-side comparison. Consistent numbering alone is insufficient. Different mechanisms may need different diagrams; one universal layout is not required.
- **Evidence:** Data, formulas, units, conditions, and sources are accurate. Distinguish fact, inference, and illustration without mixing definitions. Qualifications necessary for immediate understanding appear on-slide; other explanations are planned for notes.
- **Case-study assets:** Work backward from real-world incident slides to their image plans, rather than checking only assets already present. Plan the process, actual impact, and news/report image or placeholder. Omission requires a specific basis. Pending selection remains pending; “no unfilled placeholders” does not prove all asset work is complete.
- **Delivery routes:** Where optional coverage is planned, the complete material retains every branch, and core/short-form/skippable slides and estimated timing are clear. Read through each live-delivery route to check prerequisites, actual jumps, and transitions in notes. Do not misreport live duration because the material is long, or leave optional-delivery guidance only in an external document.

Support these judgments with specific sections/slides and evidence in the master record, and locate issues and their fixes. Slide counts, complete role lists, factual correctness, and file validation do not replace semantic review. Fix structural and logical gaps before visual production. For example: a section called “Mechanisms, Evaluation, and Governance” still needs checking for crowding distinct audience tasks into one section; listing principles for three methods while demonstrating only one still requires checking whether the others are adequately explained; “the experiment on the previous slide” must refer to the actual previous slide. Inspect these relationships without imposing a universal section or slide count.

Check roles by actual function: the cover establishes the topic, the agenda shows the route, transitions introduce sections, main slides resolve questions, the summary reviews content, the closing ends the main talk, and references support traceability. Role labels alone do not establish function. For overloaded slides, improve expression first; tiny text does not make a formally complete slide acceptable.

## Design and process acceptance

Assess the following Step 5–7 checks separately from text accuracy and file integrity. Calling a generation tool does not automatically establish good design.

| Dimension | Observable evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Generation process | Actual generation inputs/outputs exist and reference images were actually supplied. The full-deck design preview precedes final individual slides, which are traceable to the selected option/cell. Without an applicable exception, a program-generated PNG or rendered PPT is not a generation record |
| Candidate differences | With the same content, meaningful differences in composition, visual organization, or graphic language can be explained. Changing only colors, corners, or a title bar does not complete three designs. Record the reason for a single-option exception |
| Reference adaptation | Compare hierarchy, diagrams, visual focus, and whitespace organization with the reference. The design expresses its style while fitting the current content; matching colors alone is insufficient |
| Within-slide expression | Primary/secondary information and reading paths are clear, emphasis has a focal point, and graphics explain relationships or concepts. Text cards are valid where suitable, but do not mechanically turn an entire deck that would benefit from diagrams into card lists |
| Full-deck rhythm | Section slides actually display the section name and question/scope, with composition visibly distinct from content slides. Covers, content, summaries, and closings have clear roles and a unified style. A corner section number is not a substitute for a section slide |
| Hybrid preservation | Precise content compositing preserves the generated design's structure and hierarchy instead of switching to a simpler layout for code reuse or easy reconstruction |

Inspect both the full-deck overview and representative pages for roles actually present: cover, section opener, dense-content slide, diagram slide, and closing. Check different generation batches too. Record issues by specific region rather than writing only “aesthetics passed.” Whitespace quantity, icon count, and decorative complexity are not universal quality scores; relate judgments to the slide's information and reference style.

## Thumbnail acceptance

- The current c, r, and k agree with the mapping. Every slide appears exactly once in the complete overview, and empty cells retain their size. Label supplemental style samples separately rather than mixing them into slide order.
- Actual slide frames have equal width/height and the correct individual aspect ratio. Check content boundaries, not just the overall canvas dimensions. Documented pixel-rounding differences are acceptable; obvious row-height variation or stretching is not.
- Exact titles, roles, main content, necessary qualifications, key relationships, and case-study placeholders match the master record. Summaries may omit secondary explanation without changing the main idea.
- Style and shared titles/section numbers/dividers are consistent for comparable slides, sufficiently supporting style selection and overall layout review. No pseudo-text or fake news is used as filler.
- Applicable [Design and process acceptance](#design-and-process-acceptance) checks pass. Section-slide structure and style selection are established before final individual slide generation.

Fine print, complex formulas, and nonessential local textures may wait for individual-slide review. Fix title errors, missing slides, incorrect main ideas, and geometry errors first. Passing this stage does not mean all content has been accepted.

## Individual slide acceptance

For every slide, compare the current complete content record with the current image across these dimensions:

| Dimension | Evidence for passing |
| --- | --- |
| Content and text | Established wording, numbers, symbols, labels, and necessary qualifications are accurately shown, with only reasonable layout-whitespace changes. Graphical relationships are faithful and body text has not been rewritten without authorization |
| Facts and relationships | Data points, curves, axes, units, formulas, and conditions are grounded; nodes and connection directions are correct. OCR-detected words do not replace relationship checks |
| Image quality | No typos, garbled text, artifacts, accidental smudges, residual labels, or distortion; genuine scientific texture has not been mistakenly removed |
| Readability and aspect ratio | Readable at the target display scale, with the correct actual pixel ratio and content area; no stretching, body-text cropping, occlusion, or excessive text shrinking |
| Cross-slide style | Comparable titles, number boxes, and dividers have consistent actual sizes/positions. Checks include adjacent slides, long/short titles, and different batches. Parameter declarations do not replace output inspection |
| Authentic assets | Check decisions from the case-study slides to catch omissions. Assess authenticity separately from insertion stage. Image-based review drafts that will later be reconstructed retain planned placeholders; early compositing needs an applicable basis in the visual production reference. Record pending/omitted items honestly, and do not use empty boxes where evidence details are necessary for understanding |
| Design and process | The preceding design checks pass for within-slide expression, role hierarchy, reference adaptation, and generation/hybrid records. Clear content or successful rendering does not replace them |

Review records may be grouped by slide, detailing only problem regions. Recheck the whole slide after a fix to avoid losing one element while repairing another. After assembling the image-based PPT, verify order, ratio, image versions, actual notes, and rendering. Optional routes and jump instructions must be written into the corresponding slide notes. Check embedded images against final individual images, actual dimensions, and any downsampling; compare media bytes or hashes when needed. Report PPTX, PDF, and preview sizes separately. File megabytes or upscaled pixel counts do not prove clarity or generation quality. At 7D, show this version's images, previews, and placeholder materials, then follow the main workflow to determine whether to continue.

## Reconstruction and fidelity acceptance

Both Steps 8 and 9 must read and apply every relevant standard in this section: files/editing capabilities, elements/relationships, actual render comparisons, and the slide-by-slide method. Step 8 performs a complete post-reconstruction initial review, corrections, and regression checks. Step 9 takes the initially corrected version and fully reinspects all applicable slides. The two rounds share criteria but record versions, findings, and conclusions separately. Assess structure, fidelity, and final quality independently; a first-round pass does not replace actual second-round inspection.

### Files and editing capabilities

Read the PPT objects under review in this round, inspecting XML/media when needed. The file opens; slide count, order, ratio, and text are correct; notes are actually written to the appropriate slides; charts correspond to source data; and objects have no unintended overflow, occlusion, or truncation. Verify native, SVG, and bitmap editing capabilities separately. A high object count or successful SVG embedding does not prove internal editability. Full-slide screenshots covering objects, transparent text disguises, and duplicate residual letters in cropped images are prohibited. For structured formulas, verify that the target software can edit mathematical structure, rather than merely characters in a collection of text boxes. Evaluate inline variables and teaching-matrix cells according to their actual use.

### Elements and relationships

| Dimension | Evidence for passing |
| --- | --- |
| Object selection | Basic shapes and simple icons use suitable native/SVG vectors. Mixed elements are decomposed by component. Bitmaps or alternative methods have a specific basis and do not unnecessarily reduce editability or alter style |
| Icon accuracy | The specific variant, complete parts, contours, proportions, line widths, holes, and negative space match, without crop damage |
| Icon drawing quality | Curves are smooth, with no unintended jagged edges, breaks, incorrect colors, white fringes, or background residue. Intentional white parts remain. Checks on the actual background pass |
| Boxes and colors | Corner radii, strokes, fills, gradients, opacity, and padding match, without missing edges, default-radius errors, or whitening of backgrounds across the deck |
| Style characteristics | Continuous text/shape gradients, direction, extent, fading edges, dash rhythms, line caps, local emphasis, and layering match. Per-slide background compositions and crops are preserved rather than replaced by a uniform default style |
| Alignment and text | Check the relative positions of numbers in circles, symbols, icons, and backgrounds by visible glyphs/contours. Baselines, wrapping, tracking, and padding are correct. There is no text pressed against borders, truncation, residual lettering, or text/cleanup white patches breaking borders |
| Consistency within a group | Against the original intent, compare font size, weight, object size, spacing, and baselines for labels in a row, numbering, titles, matrix values, and shared components. Independently estimating each from OCR boxes does not establish consistency |
| Tables and flows | Rows/columns, merges, separators, and highlights are complete. Arrow shafts, heads, endpoints, directions, and feedback paths are intact, without unintended text crossings or broken connections. Hierarchy and intentional crossings are preserved |
| Images and placeholders | Icons/photos/textures have no missing parts or crop damage, and complex regions have no tracing fragments. Planned images are inserted with sources and crops checked. Intentional whitespace and pending assets are recorded separately |
| Reuse | Components are reused after representative validation, without confusing variants. The scope checked after shared changes or similar defects is traceable |

Judge icon accuracy and drawing quality independently. A valid asset before import does not prove correct presentation after import; theme colors, transparency, strokes, and font substitution may change the result.

Before centering, verify the original intent. The center of a text box or SVG `viewBox` is not necessarily the center of its visible glyphs/graphics. Use actual rendered contours to assist measurement where needed. Do not impose one universal pixel threshold or force different digits' stroke bounds to have identical width/height. Preserve intentional asymmetry, hierarchical type-size differences, and whitespace rather than redesigning in the name of consistency. Record baseline defects separately from reconstruction errors; follow the main workflow when changing accepted content or layout.

### Reconstruction alternatives and fidelity loss

In both rounds, inspect the original baseline and actual PPT objects, rather than only judging whether renders look good or reading an existing exception list. Specifically look for simple icons turned into screenshots, gradient text replaced by discrete color segments, structured equations split into ordinary text, special borders replaced by default shapes, and per-slide backgrounds covered by a shared image. In Step 9, reassess whether alternatives retained in the initial review are necessary and find unregistered simplifications. Many native objects elsewhere in the deck do not prove these regions pass.

For each alternative or difference found, use the existing asset/issue records to register slide/region, baseline characteristics, current object and editing capabilities, visible losses, specific reasons, actual attempts, and evidence. Handle them as follows:

- **Reasonable to retain:** Photographs, complex illustrations/artistic textures, scientific figures without source data, or bitmaps that meet the user's editing scope do not all require vectorization. Still check sources, clarity, completeness, crops, and how independently they can be manipulated.
- **Needs correction:** Unnecessary screenshots, flat colors replacing gradients, default styles replacing the original design, lost equation structure, and other obvious, fixable differences. Do not pass them merely because “the content is correct, readable, and movable,” or because a generic note says “complex,” “approximate implementation,” or “not pixel-perfect.” Meet both required editability and visual fidelity; simplifying or screenshotting to satisfy only one is insufficient.
- **Limited or unverified:** State actual tool/format/resource limits, methods attempted, and impacts. A missing library API does not directly mean the format lacks support; nor is it necessary to blindly try every tool to prove difficulty. Mark limited artifacts under the completion criteria rather than using a disclaimer to turn an unmet requirement into a pass.

Minor antialiasing differences do not automatically require fixes, and justified bitmap retention is not a downgrade. However, visible style loss cannot be dismissed as “not pixel-identical.” Group records and attempts by actual issues; do not create failed experiments for every normal photograph.

### Actual render comparison

Actually render the PPT to be delivered and compare it with the independent visual baseline at the same slide ratio and scale. Use overviews for cross-slide consistency, full slides for layout relationships, and local views for text, icons, and borders. Local comparisons use the same crop region and display scale; separately cropping each tightly and enlarging them to equal size can conceal position/size errors and is not acceptable. Use overlays, flicker comparisons, or difference images when helpful. Similarity scores are only aids: matching backgrounds cannot cancel local errors, and small antialiasing differences are not necessarily content errors.

Check planned image-insertion regions against authentic assets, while unchanged regions continue to use the original baseline. A final render copy does not become new independent fidelity evidence. Preserve previous versions and reasons for approved baseline changes. For local edits without an original image, validate against task objectives and mark original-image fidelity not applicable, rather than passed.

Distinguish the target editing software from the actual rendering tool. If native-object and compatibility-display branches exist, state which branch was checked. A valid PDF/compatibility branch does not prove that native equations or gradient text work in the target application. When the target software is available, actually open representative relevant objects, make a minimal edit, save, and reopen a test copy, then inspect affected slides. Check preservation of type size, glyphs, baselines, bounds, and structure. If the target software is unavailable, continue feasible structural/rendering checks and mark display/editing in that environment unverified. XML presence or a PDF from another application is not a substitute. Verify specific file-format operations in the actual environment; do not require every platform to use one implementation.

### Detailed slide-by-slide review procedure

Use this method for both complete review rounds: Step 8 hands off after initial review and corrections; Step 9 reinspects before accepting final delivery. The second round is not merely a recheck of the first issue list. Even if the first round found no issues, inspect all applicable slides again. Renders matching the version may be reused, but first-round visual conclusions do not replace second-round inspection.

1. **Verify this round's inputs.** Register the stage, input PPT, independent baseline, content record, and planned replacement versions. For Step 9 of reconstruction, verify that the input is the initially corrected version. Open and export in the actual rendering environment, checking slide count, ratio, font substitutions, and file identity. On continuation, reuse existing renders only if they correspond to the current PPT and the version/environment remains applicable. Do not carry forward an old “pass.” If rendering is unavailable, continue feasible structural checks and mark the visual part unverified.
2. **Inspect the entire scope and create a list before changes.** For a whole-deck task, actually view every full slide and apply “Elements and relationships.” Zoom in on complex flows, dense text, fine borders, formulas, and suspected defects. Then compare common components across slides and numbering/labels within groups, and apply [Reconstruction alternatives and fidelity loss](#reconstruction-alternatives-and-fidelity-loss); checking only text, relationships, and occlusion is insufficient. Record concrete findings for each slide, or that it was inspected with no obvious issues found. For each issue, include its region, original state, and corresponding render. Do not batch-fix everything first and then backfill the findings. Automated overflow/overlap detection, OCR, difference scores, or merely possessing comparison images cannot replace slide-by-slide visual judgment. Scripts must not automatically mark every slide visually passed.
3. **Correct together, then recheck.** Fix editable objects within the authorized scope. If reused assets, fonts, text cleanup, or tracing methods caused an issue, inspect all related instances. Re-export affected slides, then check the fix, the full slide, and neighboring unchanged elements. Recheck whether body text, formulas/numbers, photographs, or notes changed unintentionally. Continue fixing problems introduced by repairs. Slides already inspected in this round, unchanged and unaffected by shared components, may cite this round's existing evidence. This does not waive the second round's slide-by-slide review, nor does fixing one slide establish that the whole deck passed again.
4. **Verify the round's results.** Summarize coverage for each slide, fix/recheck evidence, and unresolved items. After Step 8 passes, save the corrected version and records and enter Step 9; do not deliver it as final. After Step 9's second round, check final objects/content under “Files and editing capabilities,” ensuring that the delivered PPT, previews, notes, records, and other copies correspond to the same version. Do not create unrequested formats just for this check. State the software/font environment actually tested; do not claim untested PowerPoint/WPS environments passed. Deliver as final only when “Correction and completion criteria” are satisfied; otherwise identify candidate/unverified status and specific limitations.

Validate placeholders according to purpose. News/paper illustrations scheduled for insertion must not remain missing without explanation. Preserve classroom fill-in areas, empty tables, or template space the user asks to retain. Record new image-insertion requests separately from omissions in the original plan. Do not treat every empty box as a defect or fabricate experimental results to fill it.

## Correction and completion criteria

These apply to outlines, thumbnails, individual slides, reconstruction, and detailed slide-by-slide review. Preserve continuous evidence of issues and fixes without a fixed correction-cycle count. Address factual/content errors → unreadable or occluded content → visible structural/style differences → minor local differences. Zoom in to locate issues, then judge them at the target display scale. Keep fixing obvious, repairable differences rather than replacing fixes with descriptions of the discrepancy. Do not promise unverified 100% reconstruction fidelity.

If the current method repeatedly fails to improve a defect, diagnose the cause and change methods instead of repeating mechanically. When actual capability or user-resource boundaries prevent continuation, record attempted methods, unresolved items, and impact; a clearly labeled draft may be delivered. Exhausted resources do not mean acceptance, and do not expand authorization for spending or external operations.

After fixes, rerender affected slides. Shared-component changes require checking every referencing slide. Defects caused by the same asset, style, or method require checking related instances beyond those the user flagged. For presentation adjustments, also check body text, numbers, notes, existing images, and neighboring unchanged regions; record intentional content changes separately. Within the same correction round, reuse evidence for already inspected slides whose versions have not changed and which are unaffected by related edits. Every local repair does not require repeating the full deck review. This rule does not waive Step 9's second actual inspection of every applicable slide.

After adding, deleting, splitting, or reordering slides, replacing cases, or changing the talk's scope, recheck affected sections' responsibilities and coverage, actual neighboring slides, and optional-delivery jumps. Synchronize references such as “previous slide” or “next” in the agenda, overview, and notes. Updating text files, retaining slide IDs, or passing assembly checks does not prove transitions still work. Pure presentation fixes do not require repeating unaffected content planning.

Broader inspection does not automatically broaden permission to edit. If the user limits changes to one slide, related slides may be inspected and findings reported, but actual changes remain within the authorized objects. If a shared change would affect other slides, prefer isolating a local instance.

Advance only when all required checks for the current stage pass. Record structure, facts, logic, visual fidelity, editability, and actual rendering separately. If rendering is unavailable, structural checks may continue, but actual rendering and dependent visual checks remain unverified; generated source images or source-code screenshots are not substitutes. Delivered files, previews, notes, and evidence must match the final version. Deliver only artifacts within the user's requested scope.
