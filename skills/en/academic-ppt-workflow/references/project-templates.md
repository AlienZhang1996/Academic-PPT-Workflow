# Project Records

Read the section relevant to the current stage. Markdown, JSON, or build-script data are all acceptable. Fields may be combined and inapplicable fields omitted, but applicable planning and acceptance information must remain. Maintain a single master content record; other views reference it or are generated by `slide_id`.

## Minimum records

- **New production:** The brief records the goal/audience, language, duration/slide count, aspect ratio, style, delivery/editing scope, materials/gaps, execution mode, current stage, and endpoint. Distinguish user-imposed hard limits, approximate targets, and self-estimates for duration and slide count; do not promote an estimate into a requirement. If the first draft reasonably exceeds an approximate target, record why the additional content is necessary and its estimated duration.
- **Continuation:** Read the current master record, baseline/final paths and versions, completion status, authorization, and unresolved issues. Verify the actual state before filling necessary gaps.
- **External reconstruction:** Record original inputs, slide order/aspect ratio, verified text/data and existing notes, current input checks, baseline, and direct reconstruction authorization. Mark unsupported facts as unverified; do not fabricate research records.
- **Local changes:** Record the current file, change request, target slides/objects, comparison basis, related assets/styles, review results, and new version. A complete outline need not be retroactively created.

Ask questions only for essential missing information. Record actual tools and limitations. Reference or copy inputs rather than overwriting them. Artifacts may be organized by the roles below; paths are not mandatory:

```text
brief.md / outline.md / style.md / checks.md  # Create when needed; may be combined in structured records
research.md                                  # Only after actual research
thumbnails/                                  # Summaries, candidates, grid mappings
slides/                                      # Full slides and preserved visual baseline versions
assets/                                      # Data, authentic assets, reusable components
source/ / renders/                           # Build code/dependencies, actual PPT renders
reconstruction.md                            # As needed for reconstruction/local repairs; may be script-generated
image-based.pptx / editable.pptx              # Project-specific names; identify image-file roles and versions
```

## Per-slide content record

For a new deck, include all on-slide content rather than keywords alone. Keep slide IDs stable and maintain display order separately. One concise structure is sufficient:

```markdown
# Outline
- Version, goal/audience, total slides/duration, aspect ratio:
- Full-deck narrative, section order, and transitions:
- Each section's central question/scope, relationships to neighboring sections, and slide IDs; placement and transitions for summary/reflection and other concluding content:
- Parallel topics promised by the agenda/overview → their teaching units and slide IDs, shared title and explanation framework:
- Applicable content patterns or custom organization; methods needing worked examples → principle/example slide IDs, locations on a shared slide or reasons for omission if applicable:
- Established slide roles, reasons for combining/omitting roles, end of the main talk, and reference placement:
- If time is limited: complete materials and live delivery route; core/optional slide IDs, timing, and necessary transitions:
- Source index, distinguishing original materials from sources actually retrieved:

## slide-id
- Display order, role, and section if any:
- Title (exact wording):
- Role within the section; state the central question and takeaway separately when needed:
- Connections to the actual preceding/following slides: transition, dependency, or scope change; may reference neighboring records:
- Complete on-slide wording: body text, informative annotations, labels, numbers, units, formulas, and necessary qualifications
- Within-slide relationships: how the points answer the title, their shared dimensions, or their progressive/subordinate relationships
- Graphical relationships: meanings of nodes/edges; parallel, progressive, causal, comparative, or hierarchical relationships; omit if there is no graphic
- Speaker notes: expanded explanations, detailed definitions/calculation methods, source locations; applicable optional routes, jumps, and timing; separate from on-slide content
- Source, data, and asset IDs used:
- Real-world incident case: image plan/pending selection status, or a specific reason for having no image:
```

Record the three levels of relationships with short statements or mappings rather than copying body text. Record a given transition only once. Register content patterns by teaching unit, without forcing a label or a separate full form onto every slide. If the principle and example share a slide, specify their regions and information mapping; “has an example” is insufficient. Add prerequisites or reasons for title type only when they resolve ambiguity. Do not invent a takeaway or point relationships for a cover. Omit a preceding/following slide when none exists at the beginning/end. Information assigned to graphics in the master record need not be duplicated in body text. Optional delivery routes reference slides in the complete material; check that jumps do not omit necessary prerequisites. The complete material may take longer than the live route; calculate their durations separately.

Derive the agenda, title sequence, and thumbnail summaries from this record; do not maintain duplicate titles independently. The title sequence retains slide ID, section, title, and slide role for sequential reading. Summaries retain the role, exact title, main idea, necessary qualifications, graphical relationships, and applicable asset placeholders; they may reference content not expanded within the summary. Update the master record before synchronizing any rewrite of established wording to derived artifacts.

Research may be recorded as “finding/claim — source location — date/conditions/units — fact or inference,” with unresolved questions listed. Do not create a research report without actually searching; cite the input sources directly instead.

## Style and slide mapping

The style record preserves references/selected option, actually available fonts, colors, type hierarchy, grid/whitespace, and shared component parameters. Group parameters by role, including title/section-number positions and sizes, box dimensions, dividers, and page numbers. Record long-title handling and exceptions as needed.

Also record the reference image's main design characteristics, differences in composition/graphic language between candidates, and the reason for selection. For multisection decks, register “agenda section → standalone section-slide ID.” Applicable combinations/omissions must cite a user requirement or specific constraint, not merely “short deck.”

Record grid parameters `c,r,k=c×r,w,h,mx,my,gx,gy,t,W,H` and the actual canvas dimensions. Each batch has cells `1…k`, mapped to a `slide_id` or an empty cell. Label the roles and source versions of the main candidate grid, supplemental samples, complete overview, and final preview separately; do not mix them into one display sequence.

## Asset and reconstruction records

Use the following fields as needed. Build-script data can serve directly as the asset list without duplicate manual entry. Image decisions for real-world incident cases must be traceable from the content record; an empty asset list does not waive them.

| Field | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and reuse | `asset_id`, source/baseline region, version, specific variant, referencing slides/objects |
| Authentic image | Original image/link, date, pixel dimensions, key region, display intent, caption, and notes |
| Placeholder replacement | Normalized slide position/dimensions, fitting/cropping rationale, planned extent, pending/placeholder/shown/filled status |
| Reconstruction method | Native/SVG/bitmap, target editing software, and actual editing scope; distinguish structural equation editing from ordinary character editing, and register compatibility-display branches when applicable |
| Alternatives and approximations, if any | Baseline characteristics, current implementation, visible/editability losses, specific rationale, actual attempts/evidence, and judgment: needs correction/reasonable to retain/limited and unverified; linked issue IDs in checks |
| Geometry and style | Object bounds and necessary visible contours; layering/grouping, text styles/spacing, sampled fills/gradients, strokes, corner radii, and measurement scale |
| Validation | Representative instances, crop boundaries, correctness and drawing-quality checks; linked issue IDs in checks |

Record only properties affecting reconstruction; do not list every pixel or decorative stroke as content. Mark parameters that cannot be determined from images as estimates to be calibrated through rendering. Preserve actual generation prompts, reference inputs, and available tool details. Do not fabricate models, seeds, or success states.

For new decks, record generation by candidate/slide ID: actual tool, prompt, reference images and cells actually supplied, output paths/versions, and review results. For hybrid slides, also record the generated base and precise compositing regions. Label code-based grid assembly and PPT renders by their separate purposes. Records may be derived from call logs without another manual table, but code drawings without a generation call cannot be registered as model-generated. The order and versions of the full preview review and final individual slides must be traceable.

The delivery manifest distinguishes PPTX, PDF, previews, and original individual slide images. Record final paths, versions/slide counts, actual file sizes, and slide pixel dimensions. State whether assembly or export resampled images; requested prompt dimensions are not actual dimensions. Image provenance and correspondence may be checked through a media manifest or hashes. File size is not a generation-quality threshold.

## Review and change records

Aggregate by slide and issue ID; multiple parallel acceptance tables are unnecessary:

```markdown
# Current status
- Current scope, stage/endpoint, actual tools, and capability limits:
- Content/style versions; independent visual baseline paths/versions; candidate/final PPT paths, versions or hashes, and current render paths:
- Target editing software; actual rendering software/version, font environment, slide count/aspect ratio; compatibility branch actually checked, if applicable; unverified display/editing capabilities:
- Separate statuses for reconstruction completion, Step 8's complete initial review, and Step 9's second review; input/corrected versions and per-slide coverage for each round:
- Original user authorization/confirmation, applicable version and scope; basis for waiting or continuing:
- Approved baseline changes, planned asset replacements, and applicable scope, if any:

# Per-slide acceptance
| Stage/round | slide_id | Status of each applicable dimension in this round | Findings before changes / inspected with no obvious issues found | Full-slide/local evidence and input version | Unresolved issue IDs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

# Issues and fixes
| ID | Discovery stage/round and slide/region | Original issue, severity, and cause (may remain undiagnosed initially) | Change and corrected version | Similar/referencing slides checked | Post-fix render, regression evidence, and status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
```

Use pass/fail/unverified/not applicable with reasons. Content coverage may reference the master record by slide, with omissions detailed. Record content correctness, design quality, generation process, file assembly, and reconstruction fidelity separately; none substitutes for another. Identify the version in each piece of evidence. Local comparisons use the same crop and display scale.

Steps 8 and 9 use these records with separate entries for each round; do not overwrite initial-review history. In each round, first save findings for all slides in scope before changes, then correct and recheck. Record actual review evidence even for slides with no obvious issues. Explain how the second-round input corresponds to the initially corrected version. Even when reusing the same render images, record the result of inspecting them again; copying the first round's “pass” is not a recheck. Reassess retained bitmaps, alternative implementations, and approximations against actual objects, explaining why their handling is retained or changed. Add newly discovered simplifications to the same record rather than creating a parallel list. Local tasks cover only targets and related effects; review-only tasks do not automatically authorize editing. Records may cite existing logs, and each normal element need not have its own table. Scripts may summarize coverage and versions but cannot automatically declare visual quality passed. If rendering is unavailable or evidence does not match the current file, mark it unverified rather than backfilling a conclusion.

Outline acceptance records section/slide IDs, the relevant responsibility or coverage evidence, judgments, and unresolved issues. Reference relationships and mappings in the master record instead of copying the outline. “Section slides complete, facts correct, logic passed” alone does not demonstrate narrative coherence.

Distinguish content changes, presentation fixes, and planned image insertion in change records. After presentation fixes, regression-check body text/numbers, notes, existing images, and adjacent regions. Record the version basis for reusing evidence for unchanged slides within a round. Render materials may be reused across rounds, but must be inspected again with separate conclusions retained. Also record final delivery and remaining limitations. Always distinguish independent baselines, approved local changes, and final render copies.
